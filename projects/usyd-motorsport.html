Jacking Bar Mounting Bracket

An FSAE jacking-point bracket, optimised through iterative FEA to hold a 700 N lifting load at minimum mass and displacement.

PROBLEM

A component that only ever costs you weight

The jacking bar bracket mounts the car's jacking bar and has to withstand a 700 N lifting load — but unlike most of the car, it provides no direct performance advantage. Its design is still constrained by FSAE competition regulations. That combination makes it a clean optimisation problem: every gram of unnecessary material is a straightforward disadvantage, with no offsetting benefit.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Balancing load, displacement and mass

The bracket had to withstand the 700 N load, keep displacement minimal, meet FSAE requirements and maintain an adequate factor of safety — while removing as much material as those constraints would allow. The objective wasn't to make the bracket strong; it was already going to be strong enough. The objective was to find out how much of it was actually necessary.

ANALYSIS

FEA-driven material removal

Finite Element Analysis was used to identify exactly where material could be removed. Each design change was followed by Factor of Safety and displacement simulations, and the results were fed into the next iteration — an interactive loop between modification and simulation rather than a single check at the end.

The final simulations showed negligible displacement at the bracket's connection and support points. Deformation was identified around the fastener region and accounted for in the design so it would be resolved once the bolt was actually installed — the kind of detail that only shows up once you're simulating the assembled state, not just the bare part.

FINAL DESIGN

Material removed, integrity retained

The resulting bracket demonstrates simulation-driven engineering rather than an intuitively oversized structure — every removal justified by an FEA result, not a guess.
