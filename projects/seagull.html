Seagull — AI Swimmer Detection System

An automated, camera-based swimmer detection system built to give Surf Life Saving patrols an additional tool for spotting people caught in rip currents.

StatusCompleted
DisciplineAI / Embedded / Product
ContextSurf Life Saving
Iterations15 (Mark 1 → 15)
PROBLEM

Detection needs a body, not just a model

Computer-vision swimmer detection is only useful to a lifesaving patrol if the camera and compute running it can actually survive a beach. Seagull started as an AI detection and tracking model capable of identifying swimmers inside rip currents, but the harder engineering problem turned out to be everything around that model: a housing that could be mounted to existing Surf Life Saving equipment, stay watertight, survive knocks, and still be easy enough to open up for maintenance.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Ten constraints, one enclosure

The enclosure design had to satisfy camera positioning, mounting stability, electronics packaging, battery placement, waterproofing, shock resistance, weight distribution, maintenance access, and compatibility with standard Surf Life Saving poles — simultaneously, in a single housing, without compromising the AI system it was protecting.

DESIGN PROCESS

Fifteen iterations, six documented marks

MARK 1Electronics & AI integration

The core electrical system was wired together and the AI swimmer-detection software was uploaded and tested — establishing that the detection pipeline worked before any enclosure design began.

MARK 2Initial dimensions

A Raspberry Pi and camera arrangement was laid out and housed in laser-cut plywood, letting the real physical footprint of the electronics be checked against the CAD rather than trusted blindly.

Mark 2 — laser-cut plywood test housing

MARK 4Housing & balance

The battery, relay and transformer were integrated into the housing, with a counterweight added on the right-hand side so the complete unit would hang correctly once mounted to a pole.

Mark 4 — housing with counterweight
images/seagull/mark-04.jpg · 900×675
MARK 73D-printed housing

The first 3D-printed enclosure introduced a dovetail rear counterweight attachment designed specifically to interface with the pole — geometry that laser-cut plywood couldn't produce.

Mark 7 — first 3D-printed shell
images/seagull/mark-07.jpg · 900×675
MARK 12Mounting & access

A universal GoPro mount, a slot for Surf Life Saving poles, and a waterproof charging port were added — shifting the focus from protecting the electronics to making the whole unit deployable in the field.

Mark 12 — mounting and charging port detail
images/seagull/mark-12.jpg · 900×675
MARK 15Final optimisation

The final housing combined a waterproof, shock-resistant outer shell with improved maintenance access, finished in Surf Life Saving branding colours.

Mark 15 — final housing, branded

FINAL SYSTEM

How the concept became a deployable product

The finished unit combines the electronic system, the AI detection software, a mechanical housing, a pole-mounting system, environmental protection and field-maintenance access into one product — the result of treating the enclosure as part of the engineering problem from Mark 1 onward, not an afterthought once the software worked.
