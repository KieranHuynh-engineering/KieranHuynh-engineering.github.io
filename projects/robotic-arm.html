5-DOF Robotic Arm

A five-degree-of-freedom robotic arm designed to lift finished prints off a 3D printer bed and place them neatly aside — built low-cost, from first principles.

PROBLEM

Why not just sweep the plate?

The simplest way to clear a finished 3D print is a mechanical sweeper arm — but a sweeper can't tell a delicate print from a robust one, and it can't handle a plate with several separate objects on it. A robotic arm trades that simplicity for real control over how each object is picked up, moved and placed, at the cost of a much harder mechanical and control problem.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Servo placement drives everything else

Every joint in the arm is driven by an MG995, MG955 or MG90S servo, and the central design problem is where to put them: servo position determines the torque and moment each joint has to resist, which in turn determines how the structure needs to be built. The project is being developed on a deliberately low budget, so components are 3D-printed or salvaged from household items and earlier projects wherever possible — which narrows the design space further.

DESIGN PROCESS

From kinematic layout to a buildable structure

MARK 1Mechanical architecture

The first prototype established servo placement, arm and joint dimensions, structural arrangement and torque requirements, backed by moment calculations rather than guesswork. Dual MG995 servos were chosen for the shoulder to provide extra torque, the load was kept centrally balanced where possible, and a servo was positioned low on the forearm to reduce the moment on the structure while still letting the gripper platform rotate — driven by an MG90S. Arm braces were added to stop unwanted movement while staying printable.

MARK 2Structural supports

The second prototype turns that kinematic arrangement into real, printable structure. A support ring braces the base columns against internal forces. The upper-arm support uses a circular geometry to minimise contact points and friction while reducing shear on the servo shaft. The base servo housing has an alignment indentation that also cuts down on print support material. A bearing guide rail retains truncated-cone bearings across stepped levels, giving the base five points of contact per bearing, with circular offsets keeping it concentric while minimising friction.

PLANNED DEVELOPMENT

What's built vs. what's next

Built so far: the mechanical arm structure through Mark 2 — servo placement, shoulder and base supports, bearing system.

Planned, not yet implemented: completing the mechanical arm and gripper, integrating a camera and repurposed piezoelectric sensors into the gripper for feedback, remote control, a phone app and UI, control software, and inverse kinematics calculated in MATLAB.
