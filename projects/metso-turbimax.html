Turbimax Sensor Mount

A mounting system for an Endress+Hauser Turbimax turbidity sensor on Metso thickeners, designed around a question most briefs never ask: what can actually be sourced at the installation site?

PROBLEM

A real industrial brief

Metso needed a practical sensor mount for turbidity monitoring on thickeners, using an Endress+Hauser Turbimax sensor. The brief set hard constraints: 180° of horizontal swing, an adjustable reach of 100–1000 mm, easy disassembly for cleaning, and a total mass under 20 kg — all while positioning the sensor reliably and remaining practical to fabricate and assemble.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Designing for wherever it breaks, not just where it's built

Metso installs and services thickeners internationally. A mount built around a proprietary bearing or a hard-to-source fastener works fine in the design studio and becomes a maintenance problem the day a remote site needs a replacement part it can't get. The real engineering challenge wasn't whether the mount could be manufactured — it was where and how it could be manufactured and repaired, anywhere in Metso's service network.

MECHANICAL SOLUTION

Three telescoping beams, one simple pivot

The final concept uses three telescoping RHS (rectangular hollow section) beams to deliver the required 100–1000 mm reach, using standard structural material rather than anything specialised. The pivot mechanism was deliberately designed around basic milling and drilling operations, so it doesn't require a fabrication facility with specialist machining capability. A dual spring-loaded detent system locks the telescoping sections in place using common hardware, keeping the adjustment mechanism simple and robust rather than clever.

DESIGN FOR GLOBAL MANUFACTURE

“Same concept, buildable anywhere.”

The most important decision in the project was building the entire system parametrically in CAD. RHS sizes and bearing dimensions aren't fixed values in the model — they're variables that can be substituted for whatever's locally available. If a particular RHS size or bearing can't be sourced at a given site, the corresponding component can be swapped without redesigning the mount from scratch.

That turns component availability from a redesign problem into a configurable design variable — RHS size A can become RHS size B, and one bearing can become a locally available alternative, without touching the underlying engineering concept. It's an approach to design for manufacture, assembly and maintenance that treats the deployment environment as a real design constraint, not an afterthought.

FINAL DESIGN

A result built on simplicity, not complexity

The finished mount prioritises manufacturability, serviceability, global maintainability, adaptability, simplicity and weight in equal measure — placing 3rd in the Metso × MUGS Design-A-thon against briefs that, in many cases, chased a more complex mechanism instead of a more deployable one.
