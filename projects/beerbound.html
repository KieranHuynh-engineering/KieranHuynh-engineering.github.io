BeerBound — iOS Location Tracking App

A Swift iOS app that records a night out from live GPS and photo EXIF data — built around the problem of trusting imperfect location signals.

PROBLEM

Tracking a night out, automatically

BeerBound records a user's route on a night out using live GPS, automatically detects when tracking should stop, and matches photos to locations using their EXIF GPS data — combining live location tracking, photo metadata, and venue, deal and event data pulled through API calls from a Supabase-hosted backend, with offline caching so the app keeps working when connectivity doesn't.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

iOS won't just hand you clean GPS data

The central challenge is reliability within iOS's restrictions on background processes: the app can't assume every GPS fix it receives is accurate. Location jitter, implausibly large jumps, poor network conditions, public transport and dense urban environments all produce location data that, taken at face value, would distort the recorded route.

RELIABILITY ENGINEERING

Deciding whether to trust a GPS fix

Rather than treating every incoming location point as accurate, the app evaluates each fix and applies logic to filter out the unreliable ones — a form of reliability engineering that designs around the known failure modes of consumer GPS hardware and the iOS platform, instead of assuming the sensor layer is trustworthy by default.
